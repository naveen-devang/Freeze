# Elgato compatibility: IP and legal risk notes

Research date: 2026-10-02. This is product research, not legal advice or a clearance opinion. It does not assess every country where Freeze might be distributed. No technical or branding choice can guarantee that Elgato will not send a claim or file a case; only qualified counsel can assess a proposed release against the actual facts, contracts, jurisdictions, and assets.

## What the primary sources establish

- Elgato documents a plugin host in which the Stream Deck desktop app manages plugin lifecycle, local Node.js execution, Chromium property inspectors, and communication with Stream Deck hardware. Its public SDK describes an integration surface, but does not state that third parties may host every Marketplace plugin in another application. [Plugin Environment](https://docs.elgato.com/streamdeck/sdk/v1/introduction/plugin-environment/)
- Elgato says Marketplace plugins may receive DRM protection, including file encryption intended to prevent unauthorized vendors from executing a plugin without consent, and integrity checks. Elgato's docs explain how makers download processed DRM builds through Maker Console; they do not grant Freeze a right to decrypt, alter, host, or redistribute Marketplace plugins. [Distribution and DRM](https://docs.elgato.com/streamdeck/sdk/introduction/distribution/)
- Elgato's Marketplace guidance says makers must own rights to posted content, avoid infringing copyrights/trademarks, and consult a legal professional when uncertain. It points to a Maker Agreement inside Maker Console; that agreement's terms were not publicly available in the sources reviewed, so any relevant agreement would need separate review. [Product Guidelines](https://docs.elgato.com/guidelines/products/) · [Become a Maker](https://docs.elgato.com/marketplace/become-a-maker/)
- The public Elgato SDK repository is MIT-licensed, which licenses that repository's code under its license terms. This does not grant rights to Marketplace binaries, third-party plugin code/assets, Elgato marks, or contractual permissions outside the repository. [SDK repository](https://github.com/elgatosf/streamdeck) · [SDK license](https://github.com/elgatosf/streamdeck/blob/main/LICENSE)
- U.S. copyright law excludes ideas, systems, and methods of operation from copyright protection, while protecting original expression. This is not a blanket permission to copy software, assets, or implementation details; other legal theories and contracts can still matter. [17 U.S.C. §102(b)](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=%28title%3A17+section%3A102%28b%29+edition%3Aprelim%29)
- U.S. DMCA §1201(f) has a limited interoperability exception: the person must lawfully have the right to use the program copy; analysis must be solely to identify elements needed for interoperability of an independently created program; the information must not already be readily available; and the acts must not infringe copyright. Sharing acquired information is also limited to enabling interoperability and remains subject to other applicable law. [17 U.S.C. §1201(f)](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=%28title%3A17+section%3A1201+edition%3Aprelim%29)
- EU Software Directive 2009/24/EC Article 6 similarly conditions decompilation on lawful use, unavailable information, and limiting the work to necessary parts. It limits use/disclosure of information to interoperability, bars use to create substantially similar expression, and preserves other laws such as trademarks, unfair competition, trade secrets, and contract. Member State implementation and local advice matter. [Directive 2009/24/EC, Article 6](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2009/24/oj/eng)
- Trademark risk is separate from copyright. In the U.S., registered-mark infringement and false designation rules address use likely to confuse consumers about source, sponsorship, or affiliation; famous marks may also raise dilution issues. USPTO guidance recommends a comprehensive clearance search and explains that related goods/services and overall commercial impression matter. [15 U.S.C. §1114](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&num=0&req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title15-section1114) · [15 U.S.C. §1125](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?edition=prelim&num=0&req=granuleid%3AUSC-prelim-title15-section1125) · [USPTO: likelihood of confusion](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/search/likelihood-confusion)

## Product direction with lower avoidable risk

1. **Build Freeze's own plugin format first.** Define a Freeze-owned manifest, action API, lifecycle, property UI, permissions, and packaging. Use original code, names, artwork, examples, and docs; base the interface on Freeze's needs and public platform APIs. Do not copy Elgato SDK source, proprietary UI/assets, or Marketplace plugin materials. A clean-room process and design records help show independent development but are not legal immunity.
2. **Treat compatibility as a separate, counsel-reviewed project.** Avoid promising universal `.streamDeckPlugin` import or Marketplace support. If interoperability is later important, start only with lawfully obtained, non-DRM samples and public protocol documentation; document what information was public, what was independently implemented, and why each compatibility element is needed. Stop where protected packages, DRM, undisclosed access controls, unavailable rights, or uncertain contractual terms arise. Do not bypass DRM or distribute extracted/plugin code or assets. Statutory exceptions are narrow and jurisdiction-specific, not blanket authorization.
3. **Do not proxy or mirror Elgato Marketplace.** Do not scrape listings/assets, reuse descriptions/screenshots/icons, redistribute installers, or create an alternate download path for Marketplace plugins. For third-party plugins, use only a publisher-provided Freeze build or explicit written license covering the code, assets, distribution, and support model. Linking users to the publisher's official acquisition flow is lower risk than copying files, but still deserves counsel review before productizing.
4. **Keep user-authored scripts distinct from a plugin marketplace.** Allow users to select/write local scripts and explicitly run them on their own PC, with clear disclosure that they execute with that user's OS privileges. Do not bundle third-party scripts without permission, execute phone-supplied code, or imply that user execution makes infringement or safety issues Freeze-proof. (The existing plugin research note covers the proposed PC-side execution boundary.)
5. **Use Freeze branding and careful compatibility wording.** Do not use Elgato/Stream Deck logos, copied trade dress, or names that suggest official status, sponsorship, or endorsement. If a factual compatibility reference is necessary, have counsel review its prominence, wording, and disclaimer; a disclaimer does not automatically cure confusion. Search/clear Freeze's own marks in each target market before commercial launch.
6. **Make release a legal checkpoint.** Before shipping a compatibility importer, Marketplace connection, public plugin store, or commercial compatibility claim, have IP counsel review the implementation, reverse-engineering records, all relevant EULAs/Maker Agreement, packaging/distribution flow, branding, and target jurisdictions. Obtain written permissions where the design depends on proprietary code, assets, DRM access, or third-party plugins.

## Practical boundary

The lowest-risk useful starting point is an original Freeze extension format plus user-controlled local scripts. Supporting Elgato's documented concepts or implementing independently created interoperability code is not equivalent to redistributing Elgato or Marketplace content. Direct Marketplace binary reuse, DRM circumvention, copied assets/code, and branding that implies affiliation are the clearest avoidable risk areas. This is risk reduction, not a guarantee against a demand letter, platform takedown, or lawsuit.
